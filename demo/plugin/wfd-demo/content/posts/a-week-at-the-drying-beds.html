Drying is the least photographed part of coffee and the one that ruins the most of it. A week of turning beans by hand, in notes.

Day one: the parchment comes off the washing channels pale and slippery. It is spread four centimetres deep on mesh beds, and somebody turns it every forty minutes.

Day three: the midday sun is too strong, so the beds are covered from eleven until two. The smell changes from wet grass to hay.

Why it takes so long

Dry coffee too fast and the outside hardens around a wet centre; it will taste flat within months. The target is eleven percent moisture, reached over twelve to fifteen days.

By day seven you can tell a good bed by the sound. Dry parchment rattles like rain on a tin roof.

Nobody thanks you for good drying. They only complain about bad drying, six months later.

Tigist Bekele, drying supervisor
