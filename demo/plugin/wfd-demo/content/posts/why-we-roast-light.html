Dark roasts taste of roasting. Light roasts taste of where the coffee came from. We think the second is more interesting, most of the time.

A coffee cherry is a fruit, and the seed inside carries everything the tree went through: altitude, rain, the variety, the week it was picked. Roast it gently and you can taste those things. Roast it far enough and every coffee converges on the same smoky, bitter note.

That does not make dark roasts wrong. Night Shift, our espresso blend, goes further than anything else on the list, because milk needs something to push against.

What light actually means

We stop most roasts about a minute after first crack, at around 205 °C. The beans are dry, matte and the colour of milk chocolate. If they shine, they have gone too far for us.

The trade-off is that light roasts are less forgiving. Brew them too cool or too fast and they taste sour. That is why every bag comes with a recipe.

If you can taste the roast before you can taste the coffee, somebody stopped paying attention.

Imogen Hale, head roaster
