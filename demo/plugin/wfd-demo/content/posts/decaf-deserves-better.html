For years decaf was where bad coffee went to hide. Soft Landing is our argument that it does not have to be.

Most decaffeination uses a solvent. The mountain water process we use does not: green coffee is soaked in water already saturated with coffee's own flavour compounds, so only the caffeine leaves.

It costs more and takes longer. It also means the coffee still tastes of Chiapas when it arrives, which is the point.

Roasting without the caffeine

Decaf beans are darker and more brittle before they ever reach the roaster, so they take heat quickly. We roast Soft Landing in smaller batches, at a lower charge temperature, and pull it early.

The result is milk chocolate, almond and a little honey. We serve it at the roastery without telling people, and nobody has sent one back.

The best compliment decaf gets is silence. Nobody notices, and they order another.

Imogen Hale, head roaster
