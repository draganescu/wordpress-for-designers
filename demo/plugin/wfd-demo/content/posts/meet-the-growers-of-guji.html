Daybreak comes from 240 smallholders around a single washing station in Guji, in the south of Ethiopia. This spring we went back for the fifth time.

Most of the farms are smaller than a football pitch. Coffee grows under the shade of enset and avocado trees, at around 2,100 metres, and is picked by hand over about ten weeks.

The cherries are carried to the station the day they are picked. There they are pulped, fermented in tanks for two days, washed in channels and dried on raised beds for a fortnight.

The price, in full

This year we paid 4.90 dollars a pound for the lot, agreed in October, before the first cherry was picked. The Fairtrade minimum was 1.80. The difference built a second set of drying beds.

We publish this because a price nobody can check is a story, not a price.

We used to sell cherries to whoever came up the road first. Now we know the price before the harvest starts.

Alemayehu Tadesse, washing station manager
