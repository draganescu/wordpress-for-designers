# Decisions

The record of what was decided, what was rejected and why. A rejected alternative must not come back in another form.

| ID | Decision | Rejected | Why | Source |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| D1 | Each artboard maps to one WordPress entity; the board stores references. | Storing block markup on the board, as the gutenberg-canvas concept does. | One source of truth; edits must reach the site. | `[B3]` `[U]` |
| D2 | One document with a shared core-data registry and a sub-registry per editor. | An iframe page per editor, as WordPress for Writers does for its tabs. | Writers caps at six editors and unloads idle ones; a board needs dozens of artboards. | `[B4]` `[V]` |
| D3 | Four tiers: Ghost, Snapshot, Preview, Live. One Live. | Running every artboard as an editor. | Performance. | `[B5]` |
| D4 | Snapshot is a frozen copy of the last Preview render, behind one interface. | Bitmaps from day one. | Fastest to build, exact fidelity. Bitmaps can replace it later behind the same interface. | `[B8]` |
| D5 | Chrome is one shared set for the artboard in edit. | Full chrome drawn on every artboard. | Only one editor is fully mounted. | `[B8]` `[U]` |
| D6 | Click selects an artboard. Double-click or Enter edits it. Esc returns to the board. | Chrome attached on selection alone. | Keeps canvas gestures and editing apart. | `[B1]` `[U]` |
| D7 | Block toolbar is docked in the header, as the editor's Top toolbar mode. | A custom toolbar floating over the canvas with an "Esc to leave" hint. | Must stay familiar, and chrome must stay usable at any zoom. | `[U]` |
| D8 | Header has an inserter toggle and a list view toggle like the editors. | "Outline" and "Library" tabs in the left panel. | Familiar shell. | `[U]` |
| D9 | Left panel shows the board's artboards by kind when nothing is in edit, the list view when editing, and the inserter when its toggle is on. | A fixed outline that never changes. | Product owner's comment on the first design. | `[U]` |
| D10 | Right panel shows Styles when nothing is selected, artboard settings when an artboard is selected, and the settings tabs (entity, Block) when editing. | A separate Styles mode or button. | "Styles should be when nothing is selected." | `[B2]` `[U]` |
| D11 | Changed by D49: the kind is picked from a "New" row of the inserter, not from a menu; the click that places it stays. (Was:) A new artboard is made from a menu: pick the kind (and for a template, its purpose), then click the board to place it. | A modal dialog with a form. An empty artboard that asks its kind in place. | The dialog was "not a fluid interaction". Kind must be clear before the artboard exists. | `[U]` |
| D12 | Page, template part and pattern creation ask nothing more; each creates a new untitled item at once. | A second menu level for those kinds. | Product owner's choice. | `[U]` |
| D13 | Changed by D39 and D49: everything that exists joins a board through the inserter, by click or drag; the template menu and its "Already on the site" group are gone. (Was:) Existing pages and patterns join a board only by dragging from the inserter. Existing templates and parts can also be opened from the "Already on the site" group of the template menu or dragged from the Templates tab. | Pickers in the creation menu for pages and patterns. | Product owner's choice. | `[U]` `[D:AddArtboard]` |
| D14 | A new artboard's content area shows only the stock empty-editor state. | A row of starter thumbnails inside the artboard. | "This is bad, it should work like any editor." | `[U]` |
| D15 | Kind is fixed. Device is the only artboard property that changes after creation. | A kind menu on the artboard label; "changeable until first save". | C11. | `[U]` |
| D16 | Devices are Desktop 1440, Tablet 780, Mobile 360 on default breakpoints, with the rule in `data-model.md` when the theme overrides them. | Pixel widths, custom widths, a global width toggle in the header. | C12. Gutenberg defines breakpoints (782, 480), not preview widths. | `[U]` `[V]` |
| D17 | No drafts for templates and parts; pages and patterns are drafts until published. | A "Draft" label on new templates. | C13. | `[U]` `[B8]` |
| D18 | Inserter has four tabs: Blocks, Patterns, Templates, Media. Lists only. | Thumbnails. Dropping some tabs. | C17; product owner chose all four. | `[U]` |
| D19 | Clicking an inserter item inserts into the artboard in edit. Dragging a pattern, template or part to empty board opens it as an artboard. | A separate "Library" concept apart from the inserter. | Familiar shell. | `[U]` `[D:Library]` |
| D20 | Styles has three tabs: Tokens, Blocks, History. | A Compare button; a style guide artboard. | Both cut. | `[U]` |
| D21 | Picking a token highlights where it is used and shows use counts, from the usage index. | Reading the rendered artboards. | C16. | `[U]` |
| D22 | The block inspector has a "Style applies to" switch: this block, or all blocks of its kind. | Sending the user to a separate screen to style all blocks of a kind. | Makes global styles less clumsy than the Site Editor. | `[B7]` `[D:Main]` |
| D23 | Two undo histories that follow focus: content in the Live artboard, layout on the board. One pair of buttons. The document bar names the scope and the Undo tooltip names the action. | One merged history. A scope label next to the buttons. | Familiar shell. | `[B8]` `[U]` |
| D24 | Review changes is a drawer listing every changed item with a readable summary, a checkbox each, Save, Keep editing and Discard all. | Saving from the artboard. | C13. | `[D:Review]` `[U]` |
| D25 | Board layout autosaves 2 seconds after the last change and is never part of Review. | Saving layout with content. | Layout is not site content. | `[B6]` `[U]` |
| D26 | Removing an artboard offers to also delete the underlying item. For an item that ships with the theme the offer is to reset it. | Remove from board only. | Product owner's choice. | `[U]` |
| D27 | Wheel, pinch and shortcut events inside artboard iframes are forwarded to the shell and coalesced to one update per animation frame. | Letting iframes swallow them. | Pan and zoom must work with the pointer over any artboard. | `[B8]` |
| D28 | Post lock is taken only while a page or pattern is Live. | Locking everything on the board. | Only the Live artboard is being edited. | `[B8]` `[U]` |
| D29 | Boards are listed inside wp-admin under a "Designer" menu. Opening a board shows the full-screen app without wp-admin chrome. | Replacing the dashboard. | Locked default. | `[U]` `[D:Boards]` |
| D30 | Superseded by D37. (Was: entering edit sets zoom to 100%, and any zoom change leaves edit.) | | Manual testing by the product owner. | `[U]` |
| D31 | The post lock is handled by the plugin: a REST route around `wp_check_post_lock` and `wp_set_post_lock`, and the lock dialog in `screens/LockDialog.md`. An artboard locked by someone else stays a Preview until the user takes over. | `PostLockedModal`. Filling the `core/editor` store by hand. A read-only Live state. A who-is-editing marker on the board. | `PostLockedModal` needs `EditorProvider`, cannot be dismissed and both of its buttons leave the board (`spike-report.md`, spike b). | `[U]` |
| D32 | Both side panels can be hidden. The header has a toggle for the left panel and one for the right panel, and Cmd/Ctrl + \ hides or shows both at once. Resting the pointer on the left or right edge of the canvas area shows the panel that is not shown on that side. The header itself always stays. What is hidden is kept for the session only: both panels are shown each time a board opens. | Close buttons inside the panels. One toggle for both panels. Remembering the choice per user. A shortcut per panel. | More room for the board; the shortcut follows Figma's "show/hide UI". | `[U]` |
| D33 | The inserter shows only what can be inserted in the current state. With no artboard in edit the Blocks tab is not shown; the tabs are Patterns, Templates and Media. | Showing rows that do nothing. | Manual testing: rows that cannot be used are noise. | `[U]` |
| D34 | An image from the Media tab can be dragged to empty board. It becomes a media object: the image itself on the board, selectable, movable and removable, not editable, with no device. It is a fifth kind, "Media", stored in the board's layout. | Creating a pattern that holds an Image block. | Reference images belong on the board next to the designs. | `[U]` |
| D35 | A page's template can be changed from the right panel, both when the page artboard is selected and while it is in edit. The change is an unsaved edit of the page and is saved from Review. | Changing the template only in wp-admin. | Manual testing. | `[U]` |
| D36 | A new page starts on the theme's `blank` template when the theme has one, otherwise on its `page-no-title` template when it has one, otherwise on the default template. | Always the default template. | A new page should start as an empty canvas. | `[U]` |
| D37 | An artboard is edited at the zoom the board is at, from 30% up. Entering edit at 30% or more changes neither zoom nor pan. Entering edit below 30% first sets the zoom so the artboard's width fits the canvas area, no lower than 30% and no higher than 100%, with its top-left corner at the top-left of the canvas area. Zooming while in edit keeps edit; when the zoom settles below 30%, edit ends and the artboard stays selected. To make WordPress's block tools work at these zooms the plugin styles WordPress's own editor markup (class names that are not public API), as recorded in `spike-report.md`. | Editing only at 100% (D30). Stopping the zoom at 30% while in edit. Zooming to 100% or to exactly 30% on entering edit. | Manual testing: editing only at 100% is unusable. | `[U]` |
| D38 | Edit ends when the artboard is deselected: a click on empty board leaves edit and clears the selection; a click on another artboard leaves edit and selects that one. Esc and the back button still leave edit and keep the artboard selected (D6). | Ignoring clicks on the board while in edit. | Manual testing. | `[U]` |
| D39 | An inserter row does the same thing whether it is clicked or dragged, and what it does depends on whether an artboard is in edit. With an artboard in edit, a pattern, template part or image goes into that artboard's content: a click inserts at the insertion point, a drag inserts where it is dropped in the artboard. Nothing dragged from the inserter can be dropped on the board, and a template row does nothing. With no artboard in edit, a pattern, template or template part opens as a Desktop artboard and an image becomes a media object: a drag places it at the drop point on empty board, a click places it in the middle of the board in view, moved right past anything it would cover, and brings it into view. Either way it is selected, not in edit. This replaces the click and drag halves of D19 and the "with or without an artboard in edit" of D34, and widens D13: an existing page or pattern joins a board through the inserter, by click or drag. | Click for one destination and drag for the other. | Manual testing: the gesture should not choose the destination; the state does. | `[U]` |
| D40 | In an artboard in edit, a block that is not being edited moves when it is pressed anywhere on it, its text included, and dragged. Hovering its text shows the block's outline and the grab cursor. A press without a drag puts the caret where the press was; from then on dragging over the block's text selects text, as in the stock editor. This is the behaviour of the Gutenberg experiment "Drag blocks from anywhere" (WordPress/gutenberg pull request 86911) and it is always on. It is built as a layer over the stock editor, in one file of `artboard-editor` that watches the canvas from outside and uses public selectors and actions only. The layer stands down when WordPress has the behaviour switched on itself (`window.__experimentalBlockDragFromAnywhere`), and is deleted when WordPress has the behaviour by default. Where the pull request leaves a question open (nesting, touch, announcing the mode, showing the block's type on hover) the layer does what the pull request does. | Waiting for WordPress to ship it. Requiring the Gutenberg plugin with the experiment switched on. | The pull request's research: people drag the block itself and rarely find the drag handle. | `[U]` |
| D41 | Several artboards can be selected at once. In the left panel's artboard list, Cmd/Ctrl + click adds a row to the selection or takes it out, and Shift + click selects every row from the one clicked last to this one, in list order. On the board, Cmd/Ctrl + click on an artboard adds it or takes it out; Shift does nothing there. While an artboard is in edit the modifiers change nothing: a click on another artboard leaves edit and selects it (D38). With several selected: every one has the selection outline and the brand row; dragging one of them, or the arrow keys, moves them all as one undoable step; "Fit selection" fits them all; the right panel shows Styles, as with nothing selected (this widens D10); Delete or Backspace takes them all off the board at once as one undoable step, with no dialog and no change to the site. Delete and Backspace also work with focus on a row of the artboard list, for one selected artboard (the dialog) and for several. | Shift + click on the board as well. Showing the settings of the artboard clicked last. Delete doing nothing, or opening the dialog for one. | Arranging a board means moving and clearing several artboards at a time. | `[U]` |
| D42 | The board's keyboard shortcuts can be read in the app. A "Keyboard shortcuts" control floats at the bottom left of the canvas area, with a subtle shadow, and opens a dialog listing the keyboard map of `architecture.md`, and Ctrl + Option + H on a Mac, Shift + Alt + H elsewhere, opens and closes the same dialog, on the board and while an artboard is in edit. These are the keys WordPress's editors use for their own shortcuts dialog. The dialog lists the board's shortcuts only; for an artboard in edit it has Esc and one line saying the block editor's shortcuts apply. The screen is `screens/ShortcutsDialog.md`. | A hint for Cmd/Ctrl + \ alone, in a panel toggle's tooltip: each toggle hides one panel and the shortcut hides both. Shortcuts in every control's tooltip. Listing the block editor's shortcuts as well. The control in the header, or in an overflow menu there. | None of the keyboard map could be found from the screen. | `[U]` |
| D43 | Dragging from empty board draws a rectangle and selects every artboard and media object the rectangle touches, in whole or in part. The selection follows the rectangle while it is dragged: an artboard gets the selection outline when the rectangle reaches it and loses it when the rectangle leaves it. A plain drag replaces the selection. Cmd/Ctrl + drag keeps what was selected and adds what the rectangle touches. While an artboard is in edit, the drag leaves edit when it starts and then selects as usual; the modifier changes nothing then (D38, D41). A drag that starts on an artboard still moves it, and Space + drag and middle-button drag still pan. A press on empty board released without a drag is a click and clears the selection, as before. Esc during the drag cancels it: the rectangle goes, the selection returns to what it was before the drag, and the rest of the drag does nothing. The keyboard shortcuts dialog has a row for the drag. | Selecting only the artboards the rectangle fully covers. Shift + drag to add, or no way to add. Ignoring the drag while in edit. Setting the selection only on release. | Selecting several artboards one click at a time is slow on a large board. | `[U]` |
| D44 | An artboard shows all of its content in every tier. `BlockPreview` cuts its content at 2000 px with inline styles; the plugin lifts that with a style rule on the component's own markup (`.block-editor-block-preview__content` and its iframe, class names that are not public API), so a Preview and a Snapshot are as tall as the artboard is in edit. AC-TIER-13 is the alarm if WordPress changes that markup. | Leaving Previews cut at 2000 px (OQ-11); replacing `BlockPreview`. | A page taller than 2000 px was cut off outside edit and its artboard changed height on entering and leaving edit. | `[U]` |
| D45 | The board can be tidied up by kind. A "Tidy up" control in the header, just before the zoom menu, rearranges everything on the board in one row per kind, top to bottom in left-panel order: Pages, Templates, Template parts, Patterns, then Media. Within a row the items sit side by side, top-aligned, in left-panel order, with an even gap of 160 board units; rows are 400 board units apart, leaving room for the labels. The arrangement starts at the top left corner of everything on the board as it was, and the view then fits all. It is one undoable layout change, named "tidy up", and saves like any other move. The control is disabled while an artboard is in edit and with fewer than two items on the board. Nothing but positions changes. | One column per kind. Leaving media objects where they are. Ordering by device within a kind. A label "Clean up". | Boards get untidy as artboards are added; arranging them by hand one at a time is slow. | `[U]` |
| D46 | Designers can put notes on the board. A "Note" control in the header arms the note tool; the next click anywhere in the canvas area, over an artboard too, places a note with its top-left corner at that point, selected and with its text ready for typing, and the tool switches off. Esc or the control again switches it off without placing. A note holds plain text (line breaks kept) in one of four colours chosen in the right panel, Yellow by default. It is drawn above every artboard, is 240 board units wide, grows with its text and scales with the zoom like everything on the board. It is selected, added to a selection, moved and nudged like an artboard (D41); Delete takes it off the board at once with no dialog; double-click or Enter edits its text, and Esc or a click elsewhere ends that. The left panel lists notes in a last group, "Notes". Notes are stored in the board's layout next to the artboards and saved with it, 2 s after the last change, typing included; they never reach the site and never appear in Review. Adding, moving, recolouring, editing and removing a note are steps of the layout history. | Placing the note in the middle of the view. Keeping the tool armed for several notes. A resizable note. A sixth artboard kind. | Designers annotate their boards; a note must not need a save. | `[U]` |
| D47 | The selection rectangle drawn while dragging from empty board (A6, D43) is a stroke only: a 1 px brand stroke with no fill. | The brand surface as its fill. | Product owner request. | `[U]` |
| D48 | A page or a pattern is named on the board, on its artboard's label. Double-clicking the label of a page or pattern artboard, or F2 with that one artboard selected, puts the name in a text field in the label, selected. Enter or a click elsewhere keeps the new name; Esc leaves the name as it was; an empty name is not kept. The new name is an unsaved edit of the page or pattern: it shows at once wherever the name is shown, is listed under the item in Review and is saved from there. Templates, template parts and media objects are not renamed on the board. | A "Name" field in the right panel. A "Rename" button and dialog. Saving the name at once, without Review. Renaming pages only, or templates and parts as well. Cmd/Ctrl + R, or no key. | A new page or pattern stayed "Untitled page" or "Untitled pattern": nothing on the board could name it. | `[U]` |
| D49 | Artboards are added from the inserter and from nowhere else. The "Add artboard" control of the left panel (L2) and its menu are removed, so the shell has one "+". The inserter gets a Pages tab, its first, listing the site's pages; a page row opens that page as an artboard like any other row (D39). The Pages, Patterns and Templates tabs each begin with the rows that create: "New page"; "New pattern"; under "New template for" the purposes whose template is not on the site yet, and "New template part". A "New" row arms placement: the outline follows the pointer and the next click on the board places the artboard and enters edit, as the menu did (D11, D12). A "New" row is not dragged. With an artboard in edit the Pages tab and the "New" rows are not shown (D33). The empty board's "Add artboard" button turns the inserter on. Existing templates are opened from the Templates tab, which replaces the menu's "Already on the site" group. | A "Pages" group inside the Templates tab. A picker for existing pages in the Add artboard menu (D13). One "New" tab holding every kind. "New" rows that place like the other rows: in view on a click, at the drop point on a drag. | Two "+" controls on the left did two different things, and an existing page could not be put on a board at all. | `[U]` |
